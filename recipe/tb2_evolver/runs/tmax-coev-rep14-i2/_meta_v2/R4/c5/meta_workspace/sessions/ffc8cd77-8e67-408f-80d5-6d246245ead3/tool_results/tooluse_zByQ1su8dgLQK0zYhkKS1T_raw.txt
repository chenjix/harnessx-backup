6	# Analyze — reading trajectories, extracting experience
7	
8	Your input is a directory of `<task_id>.md` files, one per task in
9	this round. No pre-computed summaries, no cluster files — you
10	investigate the markdown directly. Your output is a set of candidate
11	hypotheses, each naming a concrete **pattern** (either a gap to
12	close or a habit to preserve / generalize) and the lever you'd pull.
13	
14	Passing and failing trajectories are treated as equal evidence
15	sources. A passing cluster can teach you a habit worth locking in
16	or worth lifting into the template for the failing cluster; a
17	failing cluster can teach you a blocker worth closing. Both feed
18	the same candidate pipeline — same template, same retroactive
19	check, same global-first ordering. The bias to read only failures
20	is the single biggest cause of "right diagnosis, still-net-
21	negative round": fixes land but previously-working habits silently
22	regress because no candidate preserved them.
23	
24	## Trajectory file shape
25	
26	YAML frontmatter + markdown body. `Read <path> limit=30` returns the
27	frontmatter plus the first few body lines — usually enough to
28	classify the task. When the frontmatter hints at a deeper cause,
29	read the last ~40 lines of the body to see what the agent actually
30	had in context at its decisive step.
31	
32	### Frontmatter tiers (render order)
33	
34	**Behaviour — always present**
35	
36	- `exit_reason` — `done` / `max_steps` / `budget_exceeded` /
37	  `loop_detected` / `error`
38	- `steps`, `total_tokens`, `cost_usd`, `elapsed_s`
39	- `tool_call_counts` — `{tool_name: n}`
40	- `tool_error_counts` — `{tool_name: n}`
41	- `error_count`, `pivotal_tool`
42	- `final_output_length`
43	
44	**Eval — always present, authoritative**
45	
46	- `eval_passed` (bool) — the external pipeline evaluator's verdict
47	- `eval_score` (float) — numeric score (0.0 / 1.0 for exact-match)
48	
49	`eval_*` is the ground-truth *correctness signal*. When Eval and
50	Judge disagree, believe Eval. The dataset's expected answer text
51	is intentionally withheld from the frontmatter, and so is the
52	evaluator's textual reason — you see only pass/fail and score,
53	never the target string. Evolve on correctness signals, not on a
54	known target. For the *why* behind a failure, read the
55	trajectory body and the optional `judge_*` fields below.
56	
57	**Judge — optional**
58	
59	Present only when `LLMJudgeProcessor` ran. An LLM's opinion, not
60	ground truth. Useful for *why* a task failed, not *whether*.
61	
62	- `judge_verdict` — `plausible` / `unsupported` / `hedging` /
63	  `format_wrong` / `refused` / `no_answer` / `judge_error`
64	- `judge_cause`, `judge_missing`, `judge_lesson` — one-line strings
65	- `judge_missing_capability` — `{present: bool, summary: "..."}`;
66	  primary signal for Action-lever decisions
67	- `extracted_answer` — what the agent actually committed (the
68	  expected answer text is never exposed; do not try to reconstruct
69	  it)
70	
71	`judge_error` is the judge failing (timeout, bad JSON), not the
72	agent. Treat as "no opinion" and lean on behaviour + body.
73	
74	## The two axes of reflection
75	
76	Every candidate hypothesis is pinned on two axes. Keep them
77	independent — don't collapse the lens into the lever.
78	
79	### Axis A — Lens (what is the pattern?)
80	
81	Three reading passes over the same batch, not three buckets. A
82	single trajectory can answer more than one question — you are
83	sweeping for signals, not assigning labels. Every pass anchors on
84	the authoritative `eval_passed` field; behaviour / judge signals
85	describe the *shape*, never the verdict.
86	
87	| Lens | Question | Signals (primary first) |
88	|---|---|---|
89	| **Failure** | What blocker kept this cluster from finishing? | `eval_passed=False` identifies the cluster; then read `exit_reason ∈ {budget_exceeded, error, loop_detected}`, high `tool_error_counts`, `judge_verdict ∈ {unsupported, hedging, format_wrong, refused, no_answer}` for the failure shape |
90	| **Capability gap** | What did the agent try but couldn't do? | Non-empty `judge_missing_capability` / `judge_missing`; sentinel strings in tool output (`[SEARCH UNAVAILABLE]`, `ModuleNotFoundError`, redirect-HTML, raw bytes). Cuts across `eval_passed` — the agent sometimes works around the wall and still passes; don't pre-filter by Failure |
91	| **Success** | What habit made this cluster finish cleanly? | `eval_passed=True` identifies the cluster; then `exit_reason=done` with short step count relative to the task class, consistent tool sequence across the passing cluster, non-empty `judge_lesson` for transferable insight |
92	
93	`exit_reason=done` alone is not evidence of success — the agent
94	merely called `end_turn`. Always gate on `eval_passed` before
95	reading a trajectory as a positive example.
96	
97	The three lenses feed the **same** candidate pipeline. A Success-
98	lens observation turns into a candidate in one of two shapes:
99	- **Lock-in** — encode the habit as Configuration / Control /
100	  Instruction so a future round's edit cannot silently regress
101	  it. Justification: the cited passing tasks would fail if the
102	  habit were removed.
103	- **Transfer** — lift the habit into a rule applied to a failing
104	  cluster. Justification: the cited failing tasks would pass if
105	  the habit were applied.
106	
107	Failure and Capability-gap lenses produce **corrective**
108	candidates (close a gap). Both polarities go through the
109	same retroactive check, same global-first ordering, same
110	`candidates.md` schema. What changes is which variant of the
111	retroactive check the candidate has to answer.
112	
113	Rushing to Failure and skipping the other two leaves Capability-gap
114	bets (blocked-but-sometimes-recoverable shapes) and Success-pattern
115	lessons (what to preserve or generalise) on the table.
116	
117	### Axis B — Lever (where does the fix belong?)
118	
119	| Lever | What lives here | Pick it when |
120	|---|---|---|
121	| **Configuration** | Kwargs on existing processors | An existing component's tuning is off (loop-detection too tight, sliding window too small, a builtin's `max_usd` too stingy) |
122	| **Control** | New `MultiHookProcessor` — mechanical hooks around the loop | A mechanical step misfires or is missing: tool output needs parsing, input needs sanitising, the same guard needs to fire across tasks, final output needs reformatting |
123	| **Action** | New `@tool` — expands the agent's action space | The agent has no way to take a class of action; or an existing tool fires but returns data the caller has no reasonable way to use |
124	| **Instruction** | Jinja template edit, SOUL.md edit, or a new skill file | Capability and control are fine, but the agent does not know *when / in what order / under what condition* to use them |
125	
126	The axes are orthogonal. A Failure symptom can be fixed at any of
127	the four levers. A Capability-gap lens does not always mean a new
128	tool — if the existing tool fires but returns unusable data, the
129	lever is often Control (an `on_after_tool` processor that parses),
130	not Action.
131	
132	### Worked examples — lens × lever × intent
133	
134	The three axes cover 3 × 4 × 3 combinations; most are rare. Below
135	are representative shapes you'll actually see. Use them to
136	pressure-test your own candidate — if yours doesn't resemble any
137	row here, either you've found something genuinely new or the lens
138	/ lever / intent tag is wrong.
139	
140	| Lens | Lever | Intent | Shape |
141	|---|---|---|---|
142	| Failure | Configuration | corrective | `exit_reason=budget_exceeded` recurs while agent still making progress → raise `CostGuardProcessor.max_usd`. |
143	| Failure | Control | corrective | Tool returns raw bytes on PDF tasks; agent paraphrases and fails → `on_after_tool` processor that pipes bytes through a text extractor. |
144	| Failure | Instruction | corrective | Reasoning correct but `judge_verdict=format_wrong` across tasks → add explicit `FINAL ANSWER:` rule to the template. |
145	| Capability-gap | Action | corrective | `judge_missing_capability.summary` names "JS-rendered browser" on 3+ tasks; `WebFetch` returns redirect HTML → new `browser(...)` tool. |
146	| Capability-gap | Control | corrective | Tool *does* fetch the data but returns it in an unusable shape (binary / redirect chain) → normalising `on_after_tool` hook, not a parallel fetch tool. |
147	| Success | Instruction | preservative-lock | Passing cluster all emit `## Plan` block; if removed the habit evaporates under a future prompt edit → encode "emit plan block before first tool call" as an explicit template rule. |
148	| Success | Instruction | preservative-transfer | Passing cluster plans, failing cluster thrashes → lift the plan-block rule into the template applied to all tasks. |
149	| Success | Control | preservative-lock | Passing cluster consistently retries 3× on 5xx; removing the retry loop would lose these passes → harden with a `tool_failure_guard`-style retry policy. |
150	
151	Rows omitted on purpose: `Capability-gap / Instruction` is almost
152	always wrong (if the capability isn't there, no prompt rule
153	conjures it — covered in "Action vs Instruction" below); `Success
154	/ Configuration` is rare because knob values don't encode a habit;
155	`Success / Action` is rare because authoring a new tool is not a
156	natural way to preserve a habit the existing tools already enable.
157	
158	If your candidate lands in an omitted cell, that's a signal to
159	challenge the lens / lever / intent tag before the retroactive
160	check.

162	## Read strategy
163	
164	Your output is labelled clusters with cited evidence, not a fixed
165	procedure run. The trajectories live under `_meta_scratch/traj/`
166	as `<task_id>.md` files. Available tools: `Read` / `Grep` for
167	direct inspection, `spawn_reflect_worker(kind="trajectory-
168	digester", files=[...])` for batching when the task count would
169	blow the token budget from `TASK.md`. Pacing — how many
170	frontmatters to scan before clustering, how many representatives
171	to body-read per cluster, when delegation is worth it — is your
172	judgement call. Record the reasoning in `candidates.md`.
173	
174	Two constraints are non-negotiable:
175	
176	- **Systemic vs idiosyncratic.** A cluster (failing OR passing)
177	  earns a config change only when the same shape recurs across
178	  at least two distinct tasks with different inputs. One-off
179	  observations — however obvious the fix or however clean the
180	  habit feels — go to `NEEDS_FROM_HUMAN` and are skipped. Two
181	  tasks that look different but share the same root cause still
182	  count as one "gap" (test: if you fix one, does the other
183	  follow?); two tasks with coincidentally similar frontmatter but
184	  different mechanisms do not count as a cluster.
185	- **Frontmatter shows the ceiling shape; the body shows what
186	  actually happened at the decisive step.** Don't propose Action
187	  or Control on frontmatter signals alone — for failure
188	  candidates a body must name the actual failure mode (raw bytes,
189	  empty results, redirect HTML, cycling URL, silent parse
190	  failure); for success candidates a body must show the habit
191	  actually firing (the `## Plan` block, the retry loop, the tool
192	  sequence) rather than just inferring it from short step count.
193	  A candidate whose evidence cites only a worker digest, with no
194	  trajectory body quoted, is not grounded.
195	
196	## The retroactive check — three variants, one per candidate intent
197	
198	Before writing any code, for each candidate, ask the variant that
199	matches the candidate's **intent**. A candidate has exactly one
200	intent; one that seems to need more than one variant is two
201	candidates glued together — split them.
202	
203	### Variant A — Corrective (Failure / Capability-gap lens)
204	
205	> If this fix had already been in place on the cited failing
206	> trajectories, would the task have succeeded?
207	
208	- A commit-nudge requires an answer to commit.
209	- A template rule requires data to apply.
210	- A tighter loop detector requires the loop to be the actual
211	  blocker, not the consequence of the real blocker.
212	- A new tool requires the task to have needed that tool's output
213	  shape, not just the tool's topic.
214	
215	When the answer is "no — the agent would still have failed", the
216	symptom is downstream of an earlier failure. Trace one step back:
217	what did the agent *lack*, not what did it fail to *do*? That
218	upstream gap is the real lever. Drop or re-scope.
219	
220	### Variant B — Preservative, lock-in (Success lens)
221	
222	> If this pattern were **removed** from the current config, would
223	> the cited passing trajectories have failed?
224	
225	- A habit that's already reliable "by accident" of the current
226	  prompt / tool / model combo is not worth encoding — it costs
227	  complexity and pays nothing. Drop.
228	- A habit that was visibly load-bearing (the body shows the
229	  failing task in an adjacent cluster *not* doing this habit
230	  and breaking) earns a lock-in candidate — typically Instruction
231	  (promote the habit to an explicit rule) or Control (a guard
232	  that enforces it). Regression-probe candidates live here.
233	
234	### Variant C — Preservative, transfer (Success → failing cluster)
235	
236	> If this pattern had been **applied** to the cited failing
237	> trajectories, would they have passed?
238	
239	- The generalization must pass both ends: the passing cluster's
240	  body must show the habit firing, AND the failing cluster's
241	  body must show the habit *absent at a moment where applying
242	  it would have unblocked the decisive step*.
243	- If only the first end is grounded, the habit is domain-specific
244	  — you're about to force a passing-cluster quirk onto tasks it
245	  won't help. Drop or re-scope.
246	
247	### Why the variants matter
248	
249	The single-question form ("would the fix have worked") silently
250	excludes Success-lens candidates — they can't "work" on the tasks
251	they came from, because those tasks already passed. Without
252	Variant B/C, Success-lens evidence has no path into
253	`candidates.md`, and the round ships fixes while quietly
254	regressing what was already working.
255	
256	## Disambiguating adjacent levers
257	
258	The Axis B table tells you what each lever *is*; the sections
259	below help when two lever choices could explain the same evidence.
260	
261	### The first question — tool layer or caller?
262	
263	Before disambiguating adjacent levers, ask where the blame sits:
264	on the **tool layer's return** or on the **caller's use of the
265	return**. This single split collapses most lever choices.
266	
267	**Points at the tool layer** (Action or Control territory —
268	weigh together, no single signal decisive):
269	- `tool_error_counts[T]` recurs across distinct tasks on tool `T`.
270	- `judge_missing` / `judge_missing_capability` keeps naming the
271	  same shape (missing client, absent parser, no fallback).
272	- An existing tool fires successfully but returns data the caller
273	  has no reasonable way to use (raw bytes, truncated page,
274	  aggregated when itemized is needed, unparseable response).
275	
276	**Points at the caller** (Instruction or Configuration territory):
277	- Tool returned correct data; agent misread, didn't reach for it,
278	  or committed before using it.
279	- Failure reads as budget / commitment / loop behaviour rather
280	  than data availability.
281	
282	Once you know which side is to blame, the two sections below
283	disambiguate within that side. Configuration vs Instruction rarely
284	cross-contaminate and don't need a section; they split naturally
285	(existing-knob tune vs prompt-level rule).
286	
287	**Perceived difficulty gradient isn't real.** Template < processor
288	< tool feels true but once you've written one of each, all three
289	are "write one file, wire it into config, verify". Pick by
290	evidence, not by ease.
291	
292	### Action vs Instruction — when the gap is capability, not knowledge
293	
294	Signals that a missing **capability** (not a missing prompt rule)
295	is the gap:
296	
297	- `judge_missing_capability.summary` keeps naming the same shape
298	  across distinct tasks.
299	- Recurring cross-task `tool_error_counts` on the same tool.
300	- An existing tool fires but returns data the caller has no
301	  reasonable way to use (raw bytes, truncated page, unparseable
302	  response).
303	- Your own failure-bucket phrasing becomes "can't access X",
304	  "can't parse Y", "doesn't reach Z" for a recurring input shape.
305	
306	In this regime, reaching for another template rule is usually
307	wrong — instruction patches around a tool that returns nothing
308	useful tend to compound into more failure. Prefer Action. For the
309	authoring mechanics (`TOOL_SPEC.md`, `@tool` signature, scoping a
310	tool to a class of tasks) see the `reference` skill — come back
311	here for lever choice, go there for how to build it.
312	
313	Counter-case — when Instruction is still right:
314	
315	- Evidence shows the capability is actually present (the answer was
316	  in a tool result the agent ignored, or judge is confused).
317	- Failure reads as commitment / format / timing, not data
318	  availability (e.g. `judge_verdict=format_wrong` on tasks whose
319	  reasoning was otherwise correct).
320	
321	Document *why not Action* in the journal so the next round can
322	reuse the reasoning.
323	
324	### Control vs Action — when post-processing beats a new tool
325	
326	Tool fires and returns correct data, but in a shape the caller
327	can't use directly (binary bytes instead of parsed text, raw HTML
328	after a redirect chain, unstructured JSON blob). An
329	`on_after_tool` processor that normalises the return is usually
330	stronger than a new tool that does the same fetch-and-parse —
331	duplicating the fetch loses the existing tool's robustness and
332	budget accounting.
333	
334	Control also covers input sanitisation (`on_before_tool`) and
335	cross-task guards (`on_step_start` / `on_before_model`) that must
336	fire uniformly across every task — shapes a per-call tool can't
337	express at all.
338	
339	Counter-case — when Control isn't enough: if the tool's fetch
340	itself is wrong (wrong endpoint, missing auth, truncates before
341	returning), no post-processor recovers what was never retrieved.
342	Quick test: *can the information the agent needs be reconstructed
343	from what the tool currently returns?* Yes → Control. No → Action.
344	
345	## When a prior round failed the replay gate
346	
347	If the last round was rejected by the replay gate
348	(`_meta_scratch/REPLAY.md` exists with `ok: false`), treat it as
349	evidence not punishment. The JSON lists which tasks tripped (exit
350	reason, first failing step, brief traceback). Before re-proposing
351	anything:
352	
353	- If the failure was structural (`exit_reason=error`, tool
354	  ImportError, Jinja render error) — the previous round's code
355	  was wrong, not its reasoning. The *hypothesis* may still be
356	  sound; re-scope the candidate and fix the implementation.
357	- If the failure was semantic (candidate ran cleanly but didn't
358	  flip the target task) — the retroactive check returned `yes`
359	  on paper but the real run disagreed. Treat the retroactive
360	  check as having failed retrospectively; don't re-ship the
361	  same shape without new body evidence.
362	- If the failure was a regression on a previously-passing task
363	  you didn't predict — that's a preservative-lock signal the
364	  prior round missed. Consider a Variant-B candidate next round
365	  to lock in whatever got disrupted.
366	
367	The replay gate report is the highest-quality signal in the
368	journal — it's from an actual run, not a judge opinion.
369	
370	## Cross-round signals
371	
372	The journal CONTEXT.md renders a lever scoreboard. Use it as a
373	prior on what's worth trying, not as a rule:
374	
375	- A lever that has landed consistently is worth revisiting.
376	- A lever that has been tried repeatedly on the same cluster
377	  without flipping any task is a signal to look elsewhere, not a
378	  reason to try again harder.
379	- A reverted `hypothesis_id` cannot be re-proposed in the same
380	  shape without new evidence; different shape at a different lever
381	  is fair game.
382	
383	## Candidate ordering: global first, local second
384	
385	Before choosing what to ship, rank candidates by global net value,
386	not by the most dramatic local anecdote.
387	
388	Apply this ordering:
389	
390	1. **Global gain first**: prefer candidates that plausibly flip a
391	   recurring failing cluster (>=2 tasks) or that prevent a
392	   likely regression on a currently-passing cluster of similar
393	   size. A preservative-lock candidate with a plausible 2-task
394	   regression saved beats a corrective candidate with a 1-task
395	   speculative flip.
396	2. **Regression surface second**: demote candidates with
397	   meaningful risk to already-passing or unrelated clusters
398	   unless evidence is unusually strong. (Preservative-lock
399	   candidates are specifically *reducing* this risk — they
400	   should be weighted accordingly, not dismissed as "no flips".)
401	3. **Cost shift third**: among similar gain/risk profiles, prefer
402	   lower expected token/cost inflation.
403	
404	Every shipped candidate should make the tradeoff explicit:
405	- `expected_global_gain`
406	- `regression_risk`
407	- `cost_shift`
408	
409	If a candidate helps one target task but likely harms global pass-rate,
410	drop it or re-scope it.
411	
412	## Multi-lever rounds
413	
414	A round can ship several independent changes when the evidence
415	warrants — one per distinct pattern (gap or habit). "Independent"
416	means each change works on its own; no same-round dependency
417	chains. Ship the number the evidence supports, not a target.
418	Breadth without focus regresses pass_rate; depth without breadth
419	misses cheap wins. A good round typically pairs 1-2 corrective
420	candidates (closing gaps) with 0-1 preservative candidates
421	(locking in or generalizing habits) — the exact mix is evidence-
422	driven, not a quota.
423	
424	## `_meta_scratch/candidates.md` — required when the config changes
425	
426	Before writing the config, draft your candidates in
427	``_meta_scratch/candidates.md``. Each candidate is a section with a
428	stable **ID** (``C-001``, ``C-002``, …) that the journal entry's
429	``cited_candidates`` frontmatter will reference.
430	
431	**Header format** — the orchestrator parses `^## Candidate C-\d+`
432	headers, so every section header must match this shape. Anything
433	else (numbered bullets, "Candidate 1", etc.) is invisible to the
434	evidence gate and the round is rejected.
435	
436	**Required fields per candidate** — each candidate must include all
437	of the fields below. A candidate missing any of them is treated as
438	under-grounded; the lever choice will be challenged and the round
439	may be rejected at review even if the header format parses.
440	
441	| Field | Why it's required |
442	|---|---|
443	| `[lens: ... \| lever: ... \| intent: ...]` tag | Pins the candidate on all three axes so the journal's scoreboard can attribute outcomes. `intent ∈ {corrective, preservative-lock, preservative-transfer}`; it selects which retroactive-check variant the candidate answers. |
444	| One-line description | Forces the change to fit in one sentence; unclusterable changes are usually two candidates glued together. Phrase it as the encoding, not the observation (e.g. "promote `## Plan` block to an explicit template rule", not "passing tasks wrote `## Plan` blocks"). |
445	| `Signal` | Names the concrete frontmatter field or log shape that identifies the cluster. |
446	| `Verified` (body-quoted) | At least one body excerpt per cited task. Frontmatter alone doesn't satisfy this — structural levers need the body, not the ceiling. For preservative-transfer candidates, body excerpts are required from **both ends**: the passing cluster (habit firing) and the failing cluster (habit absent at the decisive step). |
447	| **`Why <chosen lever> not <adjacent lever>`** | Required on every candidate. Action candidates must argue against Instruction; Control against Action; Instruction against Action (or Control when post-processing was viable). Configuration candidates must argue why a bigger lever wasn't warranted. Preservative candidates still owe this: locking in a habit as Instruction vs. Control is a real choice. |
448	| **`Retroactive check`** | `yes` / `no` + a one-line justification against the variant selected by `intent` (A / B / C — see the retroactive-check section). A `no` means the cited evidence is downstream of the real pattern — either re-scope the candidate or drop it. |
449	| `Tasks affected` | For corrective candidates: >=2 distinct failing task_ids with the same mechanism. For preservative-lock: >=2 distinct passing task_ids where the habit fired. For preservative-transfer: >=2 passing + >=2 failing task_ids (two distinct ends). Single-task observations hit the idiosyncratic filter and belong in `NEEDS_FROM_HUMAN.md`. |
450	| `expected_global_gain` / `regression_risk` / `cost_shift` | Forces Pareto-style global reasoning: do not ship a candidate that is locally good but globally harmful. For preservative-lock, `expected_global_gain` can legitimately be "0 flips; prevents N expected regressions" — that's still a positive Pareto move when regression risk elsewhere is material. |

518	### The "Why X not Y" field is the main failure mode to avoid
519	
520	A candidate that skips this field almost always picks the
521	lowest-friction lever (usually Instruction — "add a rule to the
522	prompt") on evidence that points at a heavier lever (Action /
523	Control). Writing the "Why X not Y" line forces you to argue
524	against the adjacent alternative, not just in favour of the one
525	you picked. Examples of the mistake this catches:
526	
527	- Capability-gap signal, Instruction lever, no "Why not Action"
528	  → usually means the agent has no way to do the thing, but the
529	  author defaulted to a prompt patch because it was easier to
530	  write. Re-pick as Action unless the counter-case in "Action vs
531	  Instruction" applies *and you can write it out*.
532	- Control-shaped signal (tool returns unusable data), Action
533	  lever, no "Why not Control" → usually means an `on_after_tool`
534	  normaliser would have been the narrower fix.
535	- Success habit, Instruction lever, no "Why not Control" → a
536	  Control hook mechanically injecting the habit is tempting
537	  ("always write a `## Plan` block") but bypasses the agent's
538	  own scoping; argue why the habit needs to stay agent-authored.
539	  Or the opposite: a Control lever with no "Why not Instruction"
540	  → you're hard-coding a habit the agent could have learned to
541	  apply selectively.
542	
543	If you can't write the "Why X not Y" in one sentence, the lever
544	choice isn't ready and the candidate needs more investigation.
545	
546	Concrete, benchmark-specific examples (e.g. answer-format markers,
547	role-play protocols, verifier scripts, domain-specific tool specs)
548	live in the `<bench>-playbook` skill, not here — this skill is
549	benchmark-agnostic.
550	
551	Rank candidates by ``evidence strength × expected leverage``. Ship
552	the ones you actually implement — a candidate that isn't implemented
553	shouldn't appear in ``cited_candidates`` on the journal entry.
554	
555	### Cross-reference in the journal
556	
557	When this round's config changes, the latest journal entry's
558	frontmatter must cite at least one of the candidate IDs:
559	
560	```yaml
561	cited_candidates: [C-001, C-002]
562	```
563	
564	See ``journal`` skill for the full frontmatter schema. The evidence
565	gate rejects any non-noop round where:
566	
567	- ``candidates.md`` is missing, OR
568	- it has no ``## Candidate C-N`` sections, OR
569	- ``cited_candidates`` is empty or missing, OR
570	- a cited ID doesn't have a section in ``candidates.md``.
571	
572	Noop rounds (``cp current_config output/config.yaml``) don't need
573	``candidates.md`` — the orchestrator only enforces evidence when the
574	structural diff is non-empty.

576	## Pitfalls
577	
578	- **Jumping to an encoding before the retroactive check.** The
579	  first plausible story is often downstream of the real pattern
580	  — whether you're closing a gap (Variant A) or transferring a
581	  habit (Variant C).
582	- **Reading only failures.** Shipping fixes while the successes
583	  that made the baseline work silently regress is the most
584	  common way a round lands net-negative. Sweep passing clusters
585	  with the same seriousness as failing ones.
586	- **Confusing judge signal with eval signal.** The judge is an
587	  opinion; when `eval_passed` disagrees, believe `eval_passed`.
588	- **Single-task optimisation.** Idiosyncratic observations —
589	  failing OR passing — shouldn't shape the config. Log them
590	  under `NEEDS_FROM_HUMAN` in the journal and move on.
591	- **Generic tool replacement.** New tools should close a specific
592	  class; a "better WebFetch" competes with WebFetch in the general
593	  case and loses. Scope to the cluster.
594	- **Layer-by-lens reflex.** "Failure → prompt fix", "capability gap
595	  → new tool", "success → new processor". All three are often
596	  wrong. Always re-check the lever against the evidence.
597	- **Encoding a habit that was already free.** A preservative-lock
598	  candidate whose Variant-B check returns `no` (the passing tasks
599	  would still pass with the habit removed) is dead weight — it
600	  adds config surface area without paying for it. Drop, don't
601	  ship "to be safe".
