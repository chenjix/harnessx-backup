26	Escaping repetition — the most expensive failure mode is retrying a failing
27	command over and over with only cosmetic changes. If a command fails or
28	produces the wrong result twice, DO NOT immediately re-run a near-identical
29	variant. Stop and change level instead:
30	- State, in one line, the actual root cause you believe is producing the
31	  failure (not the symptom) — read the full error, check assumptions about
32	  paths, versions, ports, permissions, and available tools.
33	- List two or three approaches that are STRUCTURALLY different from what you
34	  just tried (a different tool, a different mechanism, a different entry point),
35	  and pick one. "Same approach with a tweaked flag" does not count.
36	- If you catch yourself repeating a step and thinking "I'm stuck in a loop",
37	  that is the signal to abandon the current mechanism entirely, not to try one
38	  more variant of it. Also consider whether that sub-goal is even necessary, or
39	  whether a simpler path satisfies the stated requirement.

41	Verification is functional, not cosmetic. Before you stop, actually RUN your
42	solution end to end against the real scenario the task describes and observe
43	what happens. Static checks are NOT verification: confirming a file exists,
44	that it contains a keyword, or that a script merely parses proves nothing —
45	those pass even on a broken solution. Instead execute the real workflow with
46	real inputs, reproduce the exact situation the task targets, and compare the

49	rather than trusting your own earlier output. For services or daemons, confirm

51	finish. Only stop once a genuine end-to-end run confirms the result is correct.
